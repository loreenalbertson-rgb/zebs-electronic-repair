Electronics donation program

Your old device can become someone else's second chance.

Broken and unwanted electronics can be valuable training tools. Accepted donations become workshop property and may be inspected, disassembled, repaired, refurbished, used for parts, resold, or recycled depending on condition and usefulness.

✓ Great donation candidates

  • Older laptops and desktop computers
  • Game consoles and controllers
  • Speakers, radios, and small audio gear
  • Keyboards, mice, chargers, cables, and accessories
  • Small noncritical household electronics
  • Broken devices useful for parts or practice

× Not accepted

  • Swollen, punctured, leaking, smoking, or hot batteries
  • CRT televisions or CRT monitors
  • Medical or life supporting devices
  • Smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms, or safety equipment
  • Unknown hazardous materials
  • Devices that cannot be handled or transported safely

Questions before booking

Clear answers build trust.

For full service, data, donation, and warranty language, see the workshop policies page.

Read all policies →
Is Zeb already certified?

No. He is currently completing his Penn Foster training. The site intentionally identifies that status and will only show the credential as completed after it is officially earned.

Do I pay before Zeb looks at my device?

For accepted paid repair work, the standard diagnostic fee covers the initial bench assessment. Any additional labor or parts are discussed before moving forward.

Does the diagnostic fee count toward the repair?

Yes. Under the introductory pricing shown on this site, the standard diagnostic fee is credited toward labor when the recommended paid repair is approved.

Can you guarantee my device will be repaired?

No repair shop can promise a successful repair before diagnosis. Some devices have hidden damage, unavailable parts, previous repair damage, or faults that make repair impractical. You receive the findings and next step before additional work is approved.

What warranty comes with a repair?

Any workmanship or part warranty that applies to a specific repair should be written on the estimate or final service record. Warranty coverage should never be assumed for unrelated faults, physical damage, liquid damage, customer supplied parts, or a different failure.

Are donated devices returned?

Normally, no. An accepted donation transfers ownership to the workshop unless a separate written agreement says otherwise. It may be used for training, parts, refurbishment, resale, or recycling.

What should I do with my personal data?

Back up important data before service and remove sensitive information before donation whenever possible. Repair work can involve drive access, operating system access, replacement parts, resets, or failures that create a risk of data loss.
